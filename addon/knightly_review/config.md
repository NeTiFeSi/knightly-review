Use **Tools → Knightly Review → Settings…** instead of editing this by hand.

- `accounts`: whose games to collect, a list of `{"source": "lichess" | "chesscom" | "pgn", "name": ..., "role": "me" | "other"}`. Puzzles are made from the games of your accounts (`"me"`); other players' games (`"other"`: masters, rivals, players to compare with) are downloaded and exported, and later stages will use them. A `"pgn"` account is a name as PGN headers write it (`"Carlsen, Magnus"`), looked for in the PGN files; the names of your site accounts are looked for there too. (Versions before 0.3 had `lichess_user` and `chesscom_user`; they become accounts of yours.)
- `pgn_paths`: PGN files, or folders searched (with their subfolders) for `*.pgn`, to read games from. A file is read again when it changes. Games not starting from the usual position are skipped.
- `export_dir`: when set, every account's stored games (all of them, whatever the look-back) are written there as `<site>-<name>.pgn` after new games arrive, plus `pgn-inputs.pgn` with the games of the PGN files. **Tools → Knightly Review → Export games…** does the same into a folder you pick.
- `contact_email`: optional; added to the requests sent to Chess.com, whose API asks programs for a way to reach whoever runs them.
- `stockfish_path` (required), `sf_depth` (the depth of the full search that confirms a puzzle, with every move scored; 13 by default), `scan_depth` (the depth of the cheap first scan; 10 by default, never deeper than `sf_depth`), `threads` (0 = half the CPU cores), `hash_mb` (Stockfish's hash table in MB of RAM).
- `deck`: where new cards go.
- `update_on_startup`, `update_every_hours` (0 = off): automatic updates while Anki is open.
- `history_days`, `time_classes`, `rated_only`: which games count. `history_days` is how far back to look; `0` (or any negative number) means all time. Raising it later downloads the older games on the next update.
- `analysis_minutes`, `max_new_cards`: how much one update does. An update stops once it has made `max_new_cards` new cards (checked between notes) or after `analysis_minutes` of engine time (`0` = until it has the cards), and the next update carries on from there.
- `keep_running`: what to do after an update has made its cards. `"off"` (default); `"precalculate"` keeps analysing ply by ply (making no notes), so the next update finds the searches cached and adds puzzles right away; `"unlimited"` keeps adding cards, `max_new_cards` at a time, with no limit per update. `keep_running_hours`: how long (`0` = until cancelled or everything is analysed). This runs Stockfish at full load: battery, heat, fan noise and slower programs. It pauses during live play on a card; Cancel in the progress window or closing the profile stops it.
- How positions are analysed: ply by ply (0, 1, 2, …; half-moves played), your positions first, then your opponents' with `include_opponent`. Every position at a ply gets a cheap scan at `scan_depth`: one search with one line finds the best move, and each move your games played from the position is searched on its own, so only the lines needed to measure a loss are searched. The positions where a move you played loses more than half of `min_loss` win rate are taken biggest first and searched in full: to `sf_depth`, with one line per legal move (Stockfish's MultiPV set to the number of legal moves), so every move has an exact score from the same search. A position becomes a puzzle when that search confirms the loss and the move is not a good one. Then the next ply. The grades on the game's other moves (`!!`, `□`, `!?`, `?!`, `?`, `??`) come from a cheap look at each position (its three best lines and the move played, at `scan_depth`), or from the full search where there is one. Everything is cached, so the next update carries on where this one stopped.
- A card is one move and the reply to it. A note has at most two cards: a position where you must find a move, and the position after the best move that is not good (find the punishment; left out when the game is decided there). A mistake from your games makes a note for its position and one for each later position of its solution tree where the choice matters (made from the end of the line to the start; a position that is a card already is never made again). The note's tags describe the first card only. A puzzle's id is its position plus its ply. How often a move was played from a position (by you, by your opponents, and the share of all the games that reached it) is added to the note's Notes field and kept up to date as games arrive. A position very similar to a puzzle already made (same side to move, at most 2 squares different) is not a note of its own: the old note gets a comment for each correct answer saying whether it would still be right.
- Tags: every note has `knightly`, and a key tag that finds it again (`knightly::puzzle::<ply>-<position hash>`; leave it alone, or the next update makes the note again). Plus `knightly::phase::opening|middlegame|endgame`, `knightly::site::lichess|chesscom`, `knightly::color::white|black`, `knightly::time::<blitz, rapid…>`, `knightly::result::win|draw|loss`, `knightly::rated` or `knightly::casual`, `knightly::opening::<family>`, `knightly::severity::blunder|mistake|inaccuracy` (the played move's ??, ? or ?!), and `knightly::motif::<motif>`, all describing the first puzzle only (Motifs are Lichess puzzle themes, found with lichess-puzzler's tagger from the opponent's last move, the best move and the engine's line.). Search with them in the browser, or build filtered decks, e.g. `tag:knightly::phase::endgame`. Updates replace a note's `knightly::` tags; your own tags are kept. Notes made by earlier versions are left alone.
- Notes already in Anki (the manage stage):
  - `update_notes` (on by default): notes made under the rules of an older version are rebuilt in place by the next update (keeping their cards; a card is never removed), and every note's Notes field is kept up to date as games arrive. Off: notes already in Anki are left as they are.
  - `update_tags` (on by default): when a note is written again, its `knightly::` tags are replaced by the ones of now. Off: a note keeps the tags it has (new notes still get theirs).
  - `suspend_outdated` (off by default): suspend the cards of the notes this version can no longer keep up to date: notes from versions before puzzle keys (one per game, opening or motif), and notes made under older rules whose game is no longer among your games (outside the look-back, or gone); with `update_notes` off, every note made under older rules. It is part of the update's undo step; unsuspend in the browser to keep a note.
- `include_opponent` (off by default): also make cards from your opponents' mistakes, played from their side. They go in the same deck and notes, tagged `knightly::opponent`.
- `min_loss` (default 1): a move you played must lose more than this many points of win rate, against the best move, to become a puzzle. (Later: different values for different plies.)
- `good_move` (default 1): a move within this many points of the best move is a "good move" (accepted as an answer).
- `inaccuracy` (default 5): a move losing this many points or more is an inaccuracy (?!). A move between `good_move` and this is normal play: no NAG and not accepted as an answer (unless the card setting `acceptNeutral` is on); played at a puzzle position it still makes the puzzle if it loses more than `min_loss`.

Win rate is the engine's own expected score from its win/draw/loss estimate (wins + draws / 2), so the
engine must support `UCI_ShowWDL`. Only Stockfish is supported.

The Lichess login token is kept in `user_files/secrets.json`, not here.
